The weight of invisible achievements

academic life
personal account
Author

Ivana Cardoso

Published

April 2, 2026

I am writing this post today because (1) this topic has come up often in my circles, both in Brazil and here in Canada, inside and outside Ecology, and (2) because I feel it is a reflection I need right now.

Do you also have the feeling that we researchers are always behind in some way?

We publish a paper, present it at a conference, and soon move on to the next challenge. Many times, we do not set aside time to celebrate an achievement or to process a rejection. This happens because, in academia, finishing something rarely means, in fact, being done. There is always a next stage waiting. How many times have you finished a piece of work already expecting that it was time to start the next one?

This cycle becomes dangerous because, when we do not acknowledge our own effort, our achievements go unnoticed. Over time, no external recognition seems like enough. Little by little, we internalize the idea that we are not good enough and, along with it, comes fear: of failing, of not measuring up. A fear that can shape our choices and, at times, paralyze us.

When I was an undergraduate and began studying habitat fragmentation, one of the first women I admired was the professor I work with today, who is one of the leading figures in the field. If someone had told me back then that I would one day work with her, I would never have believed it… there are still days when I don’t. When she accepted me and, later, praised my work, I was immensely happy, but the thoughts soon came: “sooner or later, she will realize I’m not good enough,” “is research really what I want to do?” These questions did not exist before. I have always been passionate about fieldwork and research, and working with someone I admire used to feel like a sure thing. So, what changed?

I believe that, throughout my PhD, I focused so much on completing stages (courses, fieldwork, analyses…) that I ended up missing important parts of the journey: time with friends, the experience in the field, moving to another country, celebrating my achievements. Now, less than a year from finishing, this feeling of not having made the most of it all still persists.

Academic training is much more than earning a degree. That is why having a life outside academia is also essential. But how do we balance this when work extends beyond the lab and the field, taking up breaks, weekends, and even the thoughts we have before falling asleep?

Balance is often treated as an individual responsibility, as if organization and discipline were enough. But what if the problem is not only personal? What if this constant sense of insufficiency is part of the academic structure itself, fueled by productivity metrics, pressure to publish, and a continuous expectation of performance?

The most curious thing is that, even when the context changes (country, institution, culture), this logic seems to repeat itself, sometimes with different intensities, but always present. At what point did we stop celebrating our own achievements? What happens when living becomes just keeping up with the next step?

Perhaps the question is not only how to get out of this cycle, but why, in some way, we learned to accept it.

And you, dear reader, how do you feel?
